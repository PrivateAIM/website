News

Here you will find an overview of the latest news in the context of PrivateAIM.

2026

  1. From September 27 to October 1, 2026, the joint annual conference of the German Association for Medical Informatics, Biometry and Epidemiology (GMDS) and the International Society for Computational Biology (ISCB) took place in Freiburg. Under the motto “Big Data, Small Data, Your Data – Transitions in the Age of Biomedical AI,” the conference focused on the future of health data, biomedicine and AI. Dr. Marius Herr from University Hospital Tübingen and Marlena Meyer from the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ) led the well-attended workshop “Developing and Executing Privacy-Preserving Federated Analyses with FLAME” on the MII project PrivateAIM. The workshop was structured into three parts: the FLAME platform and its architecture, developing analyses for federated execution, and uploading analyses to the FLAME Hub and executing them across Nodes in a live demonstration. This showed the complete workflow from analysis development to distributed execution in practice. The conference offered many insightful discussions and new perspectives on privacy-preserving federated analytics, secure processing environments and collaborative health data research. Photos: Dr. Claudia Heine (MII)

  2. From July 7 to 10, 2026, the International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Medicine (AIME) took place in Ottawa, Canada. For more than 40 years, AIME has been one of the leading international conferences at the intersection of artificial intelligence and medicine, bringing together researchers from computer science, medicine and health policy. The Medical Informatics Initiative (MII) was represented by PrivateAIM. In front of a full auditorium, Dr. Marius Herr from University Hospital Tübingen held the workshop “Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning and Analytics with FLAME.” It addressed how AI models for medical research can be trained securely and in compliance with data protection across sites, without bringing sensitive health data together centrally. International researchers and AI experts discussed the potential of federated learning, technical challenges, the transition of FLAME into productive use, future application scenarios and strategies for the sustainable use of the platform. We thank the University of Ottawa and the Telfer School of Management, in particular Pavel Andreev, William Van Woensel and Antoine Sauré, for an excellent conference. Photos: Dr. Claudia Heine (MII)

  3. From June 17 to 19, 2026, the PrivateAIM consortium met for its seventh annual retreat in Herrsching at Lake Ammersee. Partners from all 15 participating sites came together to review the results of the past year, check the project progress and outline the final phase of the project. The focus was on the final preparations for the upcoming productive phase with the coordinated rollout of the FLAME platform across sites, as well as on how FLAME can be sustained after the end of the funding period. The first day was dedicated to the state of the art: all work packages presented their current status, milestones reached and open challenges. On the second day, the “Workshop on FLAME” took center stage: an analysis was developed, tested locally and then executed via the FLAME Hub and Nodes. Using the long COVID project HALTA as an example, live demos showed how federated analyses can be carried out along the entire process chain in a real application scenario. In breakout sessions, the teams defined next steps for use cases, usability and benchmarking. The third day looked ahead to strategic topics, upcoming showcases, the long-term continuation of the platform and future collaboration, for example with the Network University Medicine (NUM). A joint hike to Andechs Abbey offered room for exchange beyond the agenda. The retreat showed that the technical foundations are in place; the next step is to make concrete applications visible and to bring the value of federated analyses into research and care. Photos: Dr. Claudia Heine (MII)

  4. From May 19 to 22, 2026, Medical Informatics Europe (MIE) 2026 took place in Genoa, Italy. Under the motto “Opening the Personal Gate between Technology and Health Care,” international experts from academia, healthcare, and technology came together to discuss current developments and future perspectives in digital medicine. The PrivateAIM team was represented in Italy to foster international scientific exchange, establish new connections, and discuss the latest developments in privacy-preserving health data analytics. As part of the conference, we presented two poster contributions: PrivateAIM Coordinator Dr. Marius Herr from University Hospital Tübingen contributed “Enabling Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning in Healthcare,” while Dr. Hammam Abu Attieh from BIH presented “A Maturity Model for the Enforcement of PETs in Federated Settings,” introducing a framework for the systematic assessment and implementation of Privacy-Enhancing Technologies (PETs) in federated data ecosystems.

  5. Ten years of the Medical Informatics Initiative (MII) – a milestone that impressively demonstrates what becomes possible when a shared vision, close collaboration, and innovative strength come together. At the anniversary symposium on March 24 and 25, 2026, at the dbb Forum Berlin, the community celebrated a decade of MII and looked back on its achievements: a resilient national network connecting research and healthcare, powerful infrastructures, joint projects, diverse use cases, dedicated junior research groups, and the successful collaboration among Germany’s university hospitals. Under the motto “Future Health: Harnessing Data. Fostering Connectivity. Shaping Innovation. Ten Years of the Medical Informatics Initiative – A Foundation for Research, Healthcare, and Digital Sovereignty in Europe,” the focus was also directed toward the future – and the next steps of data-driven health research. What began ten years ago as a shared vision has evolved into a central pillar of medical research in Germany and connects stakeholders from science, healthcare, and technology within a unique network. The PrivateAIM team attended the event as an MII-funded Phase 3 project together with colleagues from Tübingen, Berlin, Leipzig, Munich, and Erlangen. Our project partner, Prof. Dr. Björn Eskofier from LMU Munich, delivered the keynote address and emphasized in his presentation, “AI for the Healthcare of the Future – How Data Becomes Medicine,” the importance of distributed analytics for the secure training of AI models in healthcare. During the evening get-together, interdisciplinary exchange was deepened and exciting new connections were established.

  6. At the MII Community Dialog, Dr. Marius Herr presented FLAME and its role in enabling privacy-preserving federated learning for sensitive medical data. The talk highlighted how FLAME allows distributed analysis across university hospitals without exposing patient-level data, addressing key challenges in regulatory compliance and institutional data sovereignty within the Medical Informatics Initiative.

  7. On January 21 and 22, 2026, the NUM CONVENTION 2026, the annual event of the nationwide Network University Medicine (NUM), took place in Berlin. Under the motto “NUM 3.0: On the Way to an Open and Sustainable Research Platform”, around 700 guests from science, clinical practice, politics, and industry came together to discuss the future of clinical research in Germany and to shape the next stage of development of NUM. The focus was on the expansion of a sustainable research infrastructure, the improved use of medical data, and the strengthening of cross-sector collaborations. Particular emphasis was placed on the strategic cooperation between academia and industry, as the development, quality assurance, and provision of medical research data can only be achieved efficiently and sustainably through joint efforts. A large team from PrivateAIM was present on site to actively contribute to the professional exchange, strengthen existing partnerships, and establish new connections within the NUM ecosystem. The numerous discussions with researchers, clinicians, infrastructure managers, and political decision-makers once again confirmed the growing importance of privacy-preserving technologies for medical research. The event provided a valuable platform for developing shared perspectives on a secure, interoperable, and future-oriented research data landscape and for further strengthening PrivateAIM’s role as a partner for trustworthy data use in healthcare.

2025

  1. We are pleased to share the recording of the presentation given by Dr. Marius Herr on the FLAME platform, which provides a privacy-preserving, auditable framework for federated learning. In the talk, Marius explains the core architecture of FLAME, how it enables secure, distributed data analysis without exposing individual sensitive data, and outlines the path toward real-world deployment. The presentation also demonstrates a use case and the role of FLAME in privacy-preserving analytics. You can watch the full presentation on YouTube (English) and learn more about how privateAIM and FLAME contribute to safe, compliant AI.

Secure research with medical data: Our commitment to data protection with PrivateAIM (Privacy-Preserving Analytics in Medicine) PrivateAIM and FLAME form an innovative approach to analyzing medical data in which patient data stays at each participating site. The goal is to gain valuable insights into health data while protecting patient privacy as much as possible. Through PrivateAIM, we are making an important contribution to improving medicine while setting new standards in data protection.
