Personal Health Train (PHT)

Bringing the algorithm to the data: privacy-preserving, distributed analysis of medical data across hospitals.

Now part of FLAME

The Personal Health Train has become part of FLAME; the work continues in FLAME within PrivateAIM.

Learn more about FLAME →

The idea

Meaningful medical data is spread across many sites, stored in different formats and highly sensitive. Collecting it centrally creates data silos that are hard to maintain, and the data cannot be shared without the explicit consent of the patient.

The Personal Health Train turned this around: bring the algorithm to the data. Instead of moving data to a central analysis site, the analysis is packaged as a train that travels between the participating sites, the stations (e.g. hospitals), and runs where the data lives. Patient data never has to leave the hospital.

PHT was a paradigm proposed within the GO FAIR initiative. Its manifesto was introduced by the Dutch Techcentre for Life Sciences (DTL), and PHT became a GO FAIR Implementation Network.

DIFUTURE Tübingen joined the PHT Implementation Network in 2018 and developed a deployment-ready, fully open-source implementation, PHT-meDIC. It was built on container technology, so that even complex workflows such as genomics pipelines or deep-learning algorithms could travel between sites.

Within the Medical Informatics Initiative, Data Integration Centers (DICs) are being introduced at each medical center as local hubs providing secure access to integrated data.

How it worked

The Tübingen implementation relied on a set of central services to submit, route and control trains: researchers submitted and signed their analyses through a central user interface, a train builder packaged them, and a train router moved each train along its route from station to station.

Every train had to be manually reviewed and accepted at a station before it was allowed to run. Execution at a station was split into three phases:

  1. Pre-run security check: the algorithm is validated against manipulation and the model is decrypted using envelope encryption.
  2. Execution: the train image, including all software requirements wrapped in the container, runs locally at the station.
  3. Post-run encryption: the results are encrypted and the train is prepared for departure.

Results were only ever stored centrally in encrypted form and had to be decrypted locally.
